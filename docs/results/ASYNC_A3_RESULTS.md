## Q2 — Do speed differences produce persistent acquisition differences?

**Yes, and the mechanism is now fully reconstructed.** Stated precisely:

> **Within this frozen ecology, relative inference timing causally determines
> the allocation of losses generated by an existing contention topology.**

Contention determines *how much* conflict exists. Timing determines *who pays*.
That is stronger and narrower than "fast models do better", and it is what the
data supports.

### What vanished (therefore: caused by speed)

**Cognition opportunities.** In NATURAL the fast model simply thinks more often.

```
                NATURAL          EQUALIZED
lfm2.5          544 actions      200
qwen3.5         268              200
falcon          270              200
```

**First-arrival share.** Who lands first in a contested tick.

```
                NATURAL          EQUALIZED
lfm2.5          55.4%            26.5%
falcon          21.6%            35.5%
qwen3.5         23.0%            38.0%
```

**Who pays for asynchrony.** This is the sharpest result in the run. Under
natural timing, staleness falls almost entirely on the slow model; under
equalization it splits between the two agents that actually contend.

```
STALE_CONFLICT by agent
                NATURAL          EQUALIZED
lfm2.5            5              104
qwen3.5         187               94
falcon            6                0
                ---              ---
                198              198
```

qwen3.5 absorbs **94% of all staleness** in NATURAL (187 of 198) and **47%**
under equalization. The total is identical in both arms; only its distribution
moves. Per the pre-committed reading: *a difference that vanishes in arm 3 is
explained by speed.* This one does, and the mechanism is visible — the slow
model's observations are 2 ticks old when they land, the fast model's are 0.

### What survived (therefore: not speed)

Acquisition counts do **not** equalize:

```
acquisitions        NATURAL              EQUALIZED
lfm2.5              538 / 544 = 98.9%     96 / 200 = 48.0%
qwen3.5              81 / 268 = 30.2%    106 / 200 = 53.0%
falcon              262 / 270 = 97.0%    200 / 200 = 100.0%
spread              457                  104
gini                0.3458               0.1725
```

Under equalization all three agents have identical opportunity (200) and
identical release timing (age exactly 4), so the residual difference **cannot**
be latency. Its cause is in the next section, and it is not a property this
experiment set out to measure.

## THE EFFECT MODIFIER — contention topology

The three models have **stable selection preferences over the presented
choices**, and those preferences barely overlap. From SERIAL, where nothing
contends for cognition time:

```
lfm2.5    r_00 200, r_01 200, r_02 200, r_03 200     (exactly 200 each)
falcon    r_05 200, r_07 200, r_09 200, r_12 200     (exactly 200 each)
qwen3.5   r_00 200, r_06 200, r_08 200, r_02 199
```

That is a deterministic cycle, not a rich choice. lfm2.5 lives on
`r_00..r_03`, falcon on `r_05..r_12`.

**What drives that stability is NOT yet separated.** Identifier preference,
list-position preference, availability-pattern preference and transition/cycling
behaviour are all consistent with the same frequency table. See the topology
section below, which measures them apart instead of assuming one.

The consequence is that **the entire staleness signal is one pair of models
colliding.** In EQUALIZED:

```
lfm2.5   r_00 (100), r_01 (100)              104 stale
qwen3.5  r_00  (99), r_01  (99)               94 stale
falcon   r_07 (100), r_08  (98)                0 stale
```

falcon contends with nobody, so it accepts 200 of 200. lfm2.5 and qwen3.5 fight
head-on over `r_00` and `r_01` and split the difference. falcon's 100% is not
skill, speed, or scheduling — it is an empty lane.

**This is not a confound for Q2.** Target selection is fixed across NATURAL and
EQUALIZED — it does not vary with the timing treatment — so it cannot by itself
manufacture the arm difference. It is an **effect modifier**: it decides where
timing has anything to act on.

```text
MODEL-SPECIFIC SELECTION
          |
   who collides with whom
          |
   CONTENTION GRAPH
          |
        timing
          |
   who wins / who becomes stale
```

falcon owning an empty lane does not invalidate the lfm2.5/qwen3.5 timing
effect. It shows where the effect can and cannot appear.

**What it does limit is generality.** `18.30%` is emphatically not "the
stale-conflict rate of asynchronous inference". It is the stale rate of:

```text
these models
+ these target-selection tendencies
+ this resource topology
+ this world pressure
+ this timing regime
```

A perfectly good result, just narrower.

### The contention graph, built mechanically

Edge weight = simultaneous same-target selections / simultaneous opportunities,
where "simultaneous" means both agents formed their view at the same
`observed_tick`. Computed from the frozen journals by
`tools/async_contention_topology.py`. **No new model calls.**

```
                 SERIAL          NATURAL          EQUALIZED
lfm2.5-qwen3.5   400/800 50.0%   38/77   49.4%    198/200  99.0%
lfm2.5-falcon      0/800  0.0%    0/213   0.0%      0/200   0.0%
qwen3.5-falcon     0/800  0.0%    3/38    7.9%      0/200   0.0%
```

One freeway and two abandoned county roads, in every arm. The edge is a
property of the roster, not of the timing regime.

### The mechanism, closed at 100%

For each STALE_CONFLICT, was there an ACCEPTED action on the same target
applied in `[observed_tick, applied_tick]` by someone else?

```
                 stale   explained    who took it first
NATURAL           198    198 = 100%   lfm2.5 165 (83.3%), falcon 22, qwen3.5 11
EQUALIZED         198    198 = 100%   qwen3.5 104 (52.5%), lfm2.5 94 (47.5%)
```

**Every single stale event in both arms is mechanically accounted for by "same
target, someone took it first".** This is no longer an observed arm difference;
it is a reconstructed mechanism. Under natural timing lfm2.5 wins 83% of those
races. Under equalization the same races split 53/47.

The window has to *include* the observation tick. A first pass used
`(observed_tick, applied_tick]` and explained 100% of EQUALIZED but only 16.7%
of NATURAL — the predictor was wrong, not NATURAL mysterious. The world marks a
target stale on `invalidated_at_version > observation.version`, a version
comparison, and under NATURAL the fast agent frequently takes the resource
*inside the observing agent's own tick*.

### Identifier preference vs list-position preference

Not adjudicated, but separated. A position-driven model holds a rank constant
while the ids under it change; an identifier-driven model holds ids constant
while their rank moves.

```
             top-1 RANK share          top-1 ID share
             SER   NAT   EQU           SER   NAT   EQU
lfm2.5       1.00  1.00  1.00          0.25  0.32  0.50
qwen3.5      0.50  0.65  0.99          0.25  0.24  0.49
falcon       0.50  0.71  0.99          0.25  0.47  0.50
```

lfm2.5 chose the **rank-0 item on every one of its 1,544 cognitions across all
three arms**. Its apparent affection for `r_00..r_03` is entirely explained by
always taking the head of the list. falcon's rank moves (2, 5, 3 in SERIAL)
while its ids stay fixed at exactly 200 each, which is the opposite signature.

That makes the freeway edge interesting: lfm2.5 and qwen3.5 collide because both
are drawn toward the **head of the presented list**, not because they
independently love `r_00`. This is observational separation across three arms,
not a manipulation — a manipulation is what ASYNC-B is for.

### Reported, not repaired

Diversifying targets, randomising the visible list, or selecting models by
preference spread would each be a new experiment with its own pre-registration.
Doing any of them now, after seeing the numbers, is exactly the move the
protocol forbids.

## Q3 — Does arrival order alone change outcomes?

Counterfactual replay over NATURAL's corpus, latency-rank inversion within
simultaneity groups. Source corpus unmutated (`source_envelopes_mutated: 0`),
zero model calls.

```
reorderable groups     296
envelopes reordered    370
pairwise inversions    185
journal_differs        true

                  NATURAL   ORDER_REPLAY
ACCEPTED              881            881
STALE_CONFLICT        198            195
SEMANTIC_INVALID        2              4
CONTENTION_LOST         1              2
final_world_hash  6551ed240a32a7a4 == 6551ed240a32a7a4
```

185 pairwise inversions moved **5 actions of 1082**, none of them into or out of
ACCEPTED, and the final holder map is bit-identical.

The claim stays small. A2's ORDER_REPLAY produced the same shape (186
inversions, 1 action moved, identical final hash), but that is **qualitative
recurrence in an earlier quarantined trajectory**, not an independent
replication: A2's source NATURAL never passed the runtime-integrity instrument,
so it cannot upgrade the evidence grade. A3 is the valid result.

> In A3, substantial application-order inversion altered the detailed journal
> but changed only five outcome classifications, none crossing the
> accepted/non-accepted boundary, and left the measured final holder state
> unchanged.

A good null — and the topology explains it. **Ordering only matters where
actions actually compete.** Two of the three pairs never contend at all, so most
of the inversion was moving cars around on different roads.

## What is deliberately NOT claimed

- **No model quality claim.** falcon's 100% acceptance measures an uncontested
  lane, not capability.
- **n = 1 per arm.** No variance estimate, no significance testing. The
  qwen3.5 staleness shift (94% → 47%) is large and mechanically explained, but
  it is one replicate.
- **No emergence claim.** The agents are deterministic cyclers under this
  contract. Nothing here is adaptation.
- **A2 is not revalidated.** A3 answering the question does not make A2's arms
  confirmatory; they ran under a defective instrument.
